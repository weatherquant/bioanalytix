---
title: Asset allocation through retirement
description: How longevity, withdrawals, inflation and sequence risk interact with investment strategy through retirement.
---

Asset allocation describes how a portfolio is divided among different types of investments, such as growth assets, defensive assets and cash.

There is no single allocation that is appropriate for every retirement.

The appropriate trade-offs depend on objectives, risk tolerance, capacity for loss, other resources, spending requirements and investment horizon.

Longevity can materially affect that horizon.

## Growth and defensive assets

Growth assets, such as equities, generally offer greater long-term growth potential but can experience substantial short-term volatility.

Defensive assets, such as high-quality fixed income and cash, generally provide greater stability and liquidity but may offer lower long-term expected returns.

Retirement planning involves balancing these competing characteristics.

## Why longevity matters

A person expecting to fund a long retirement may retain a substantial investment horizon even after leaving work.

An allocation that becomes excessively defensive too early may reduce short-term volatility but can increase exposure to other risks, including:

- inflation;
- insufficient long-term growth; and
- depletion of assets during a long retirement.

Conversely, maintaining substantial exposure to volatile growth assets can increase the consequences of market losses when withdrawals are occurring.

The issue is therefore not simply whether a portfolio is "conservative" or "aggressive."

It is whether its risks are appropriate for the financial problem being solved.

## Sequence risk and liquidity

Early retirement is particularly sensitive to the combination of market losses and withdrawals.

One way to explore that risk is to distinguish between assets required for nearer-term spending and assets intended to support later years.

Liquidity can provide flexibility during periods when selling volatile assets would be undesirable.

But holding excessive cash for long periods can introduce inflation and opportunity costs.

These are trade-offs rather than universal rules.

## Asset allocation can evolve

A retirement portfolio does not necessarily need to maintain the same allocation forever.

Possible approaches may explore changing allocations through time as:

- retirement begins;
- spending needs evolve;
- the remaining investment horizon changes;
- market conditions affect the portfolio;
- health or care requirements change; or
- estate objectives become more important.

Such changes are sometimes described through **glidepaths**.

A glidepath can become more defensive through time, maintain a relatively stable allocation, or under some strategies change in other ways.

Bioanalytix should model these as alternatives rather than assume one glidepath is universally superior.

## Withdrawal policy and allocation belong together

Asset allocation cannot be evaluated independently of withdrawals.

A portfolio supporting flexible spending may tolerate risks differently from one required to fund a rigid level of expenditure.

Likewise, other sources of reliable income can affect how much portfolio risk a household is able or willing to accept.

Bioanalytix can therefore explore combinations of:

**longevity scenario + spending path + withdrawal policy + asset allocation**

rather than treating each decision in isolation.

## From longevity evidence to investment scenarios

This is where Bioanalytix can eventually make an important connection.

If evidence changes the range of longevity scenarios worth considering, the platform can test whether different investment strategies remain financially resilient across those horizons.

That does **not** mean:

**genetic variant → buy or sell an investment**

It means:

**evidence → longevity scenario → financial horizon → portfolio scenario**

The distinction is fundamental.

Bioanalytix provides planning analysis and scenario exploration. It does not provide an individualized recommendation to buy, sell or hold a particular security or financial product.
