---
title: Retirement planning
description: How longevity uncertainty affects retirement timing, spending and portfolio sustainability.
---

Retirement planning connects several uncertain variables:

- when work will stop;
- how much will be spent;
- how investment markets will perform;
- how inflation will evolve;
- how health and care needs may change; and
- how long retirement will last.

These risks interact.

Bioanalytix is designed to make those interactions easier to explore.

## Retirement age changes more than one variable

Retiring earlier can have two simultaneous effects.

It may reduce the number of years available to accumulate retirement assets while increasing the number of years those assets need to support spending.

For example, moving retirement from age 67 to 61 does not simply shift a date by six years. It can mean:

**six fewer years of accumulation + six additional years of retirement funding**

before considering investment returns, taxation or other effects.

## Spending is not necessarily constant

Retirement spending can change over time.

Some households may spend more during active early retirement, less during later years, and potentially more again if healthcare or care requirements increase.

A useful planning model should therefore be able to explore different spending paths rather than assume one fixed real amount forever.

## Sustainable income

The central question is not simply:

**How much can I withdraw this year?**

It is:

**What level of spending can the plan support across a range of plausible future conditions?**

That requires considering longevity, investment returns, inflation, spending flexibility and other resources together.

## Sequence-of-returns risk

Average investment return does not tell the whole story once withdrawals begin.

Two portfolios can earn the same average return over retirement but produce very different outcomes depending on **when** strong and weak returns occur.

Poor returns early in retirement can be particularly damaging because withdrawals remove capital before the portfolio has an opportunity to recover.

This is known as **sequence-of-returns risk**.

## Flexibility can matter

Retirement plans do not always need to follow a rigid withdrawal path.

Possible scenarios can explore:

- temporarily reducing discretionary spending;
- changing withdrawal rates;
- altering retirement timing;
- maintaining liquidity reserves;
- adjusting investment exposure;
- using other income sources; and
- revisiting estate objectives.

Bioanalytix can use scenario analysis to show the consequences of these choices without pretending that one strategy is correct for every household.

## Genetics does not determine retirement

Genetic evidence should never be used to tell somebody when to retire.

Its role is much narrower.

If biological evidence contributes to a different view of health or longevity uncertainty, Bioanalytix can test whether the financial plan remains robust under the corresponding scenarios.

The decision remains human.

The model helps illuminate the trade-offs.
